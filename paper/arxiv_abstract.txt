Edge-IIoTset is the reference benchmark for machine-learning intrusion
detection in the industrial IoT, and the literature built on it reports
accuracies above 99%. Much of that performance is not intrusion detection. The
preprocessing recipe distributed with the dataset instructs researchers to
one-hot encode seven categorical columns. Four of them recover
the attack/normal label with an accuracy of 1.0000 on their own, through
nothing but the spelling of the placeholder written for an absent protocol
field: normal-traffic and attack captures were parsed separately, so one
branch wrote '0' and the other '0.0'. The label is thus recoverable from a
serialisation artifact encoding file provenance, with no network behaviour
modelled. It separates every row of both curated subsets (157,800 and
2,219,201). Under 5-fold x 3-repeat cross-validation all six standard
classifiers, plus an MLP and a 1D-CNN, attain 1.0000 +/- 0.0000 accuracy in
all fifteen folds - including Gaussian naive Bayes, far too weak to solve the
task genuinely. Under a corrected protocol naive Bayes falls by 0.3005
macro-F1 and the best model settles at 0.9503. The leak is encoding-agnostic:
label, ordinal and frequency encoding all give the same result.

The curated subsets also cannot support agricultural work: Modbus is
effectively absent and device identity stripped. We rebuild from the raw
captures under uniform parsing, producing AgriEdge: 1,276,122 rows over five
devices with full attribution and no column separating the classes above
0.0288. A leave-one-device-out sweep locates the failure boundary: transfer
between perception sensors is nearly free, but collapses across the
perception/actuation layer, with random forest falling from 0.9988 to 0.5083
when the Modbus gateway is withheld. Non-IID federated partitioning costs at
most 0.0037 macro-F1, while the LoRaWAN uplink to train a 28,450-parameter
model costs 4.6 hours. Code is released.
